# References and evidence notes

This protocol combines material from the uploaded *ISHA Monograph: Evaluation and Management of Vestibular Dysfunction* with published research. It is a draft for clinician review, not a validated treatment guideline.

## Source-derived material: uploaded ISHA Monograph
- **Kumari Apeksha & Niraj Kumar Singh.** “Non-medical management of vestibular disorders” / vestibular rehabilitation section. *ISHA Monograph: Evaluation and Management of Vestibular Dysfunction* (2018), pp. 249–254. Covers Cawthorne–Cooksey progression, gaze stabilization, VOR X1/X2 and substitution exercises.
- **Savitha Kamath.** “Professionals involved in assessment and management of balance dysfunction.” Same monograph, pp. 282–289. Discusses multidisciplinary care, migraine trigger/lifestyle counselling, habituation and balance exercises, relaxation and breathing.
- **Swarup Bikash Mishra.** Vestibular migraine chapter, including diagnostic features and differential diagnosis, around pp. 209–212.

The monograph provides general vestibular rehabilitation concepts; it does not validate every exercise or dose as a vestibular-migraine-specific protocol.

## Published literature
1. **Effects of Vestibular Rehabilitation in the Management of a Vestibular Migraine: A Review.** 2018. Open-access review. Describes gaze stability, habituation, gait/balance training and physical activity; notes that the evidence available at the time was limited and studies had risk of bias.  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6005864/

2. **Hall CD, Herdman SJ, Whitney SL, et al.** Vestibular Rehabilitation for Peripheral Vestibular Hypofunction: An Updated Clinical Practice Guideline From the Academy of Neurologic Physical Therapy of the American Physical Therapy Association. *Journal of Neurologic Physical Therapy*. 2022;46(2):118–177.  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8920012/  
   **Applicability note:** This guideline is for peripheral vestibular hypofunction, not vestibular migraine alone. Its exercise categories and safety principles may inform clinical reasoning, but its dose recommendations should not be transferred directly to VM.

3. **Virtual Reality-Based Vestibular Rehabilitation for Vestibular Migraine: A Randomized Trial.** *The Laryngoscope*, online 2026. Reports an individualized four-week home programme with adaptation, habituation and substitution/balance exercises; VR was an additional supervised intervention in the experimental arm. Intensity was adjusted for symptom tolerance.  
   https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/lary.70814  
   **Applicability note:** This recent trial is evidence of a studied approach, not proof that its exact schedule suits every patient. Review the full paper and population before adapting its methods.

4. **The Effectiveness of Vestibular Rehabilitation in Vestibular Migraine: A Systematic Review.** Published 2026; search period through September 30, 2024. Reports improvement in several outcomes across 11 studies, while identifying risk of bias and the need for more standardised research.  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41840297/

5. **Vestibular Rehabilitation for Vestibular Migraine: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.** Published 2026. Seven studies (272 participants) were included; the authors reported improved Dizziness Handicap Inventory scores but substantial heterogeneity and limited/moderate-to-low certainty.  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42709655/

## Evidence interpretation
- Evidence supports considering vestibular rehabilitation as one component of care for adults with vestibular migraine, but it does not establish one universal exercise set or dose.
- The correct components depend on assessment: gaze instability, motion sensitivity, visually induced dizziness, gait/balance impairment and functional goals.
- Progression should be individualised, monitored, and coordinated with medical management of migraine.
- The animations in `index.html` are original schematic SVG illustrations and are not copied from the source publications.
- References should be rechecked by the repository maintainer before clinical publication; this project is not a substitute for a systematic review.

## Implementation note
The browser visual stimulus lab uses Canvas-rendered target motion and a low-contrast moving dot field with user-adjustable pixel speed, range/size, contrast, and spacing. These are demonstration controls, not calibrated clinical stimuli; screen pixel speed is not equivalent to degrees per second. The page does not store or transmit symptom-log entries.
